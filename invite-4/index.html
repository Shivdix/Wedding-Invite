Tradition

Mexican

Mexican wedding traditions are filled with meaning, joy and community. These customs symbolize love, protection, good fortune and the union of two families.

They bring energy, laughter and togetherness to the celebration, while also honoring our heritage and the importance of family and friends.

We are excited to share these special traditions with you and hope you enjoy being part of this vibrant celebration!

“La alegría de nuestras tradiciones nos recuerda que el amor es más grande cuando se comparte.”

Mexican Proverb

Mexican Traditions

EL MUERTITO

A lively and symbolic tradition where the groom is lifted, carried and tossed into the air by his friends and male relatives. It represents the “death” of his single life and his transition into a married man.

  • Male guests lift the groom as if carrying a casket in a fun, celebratory way.
  • He is tossed in the air and caught to the beat of music.
  • Guests often remove his shoes.
  • It’s a high-energy, humorous tradition that symbolizes the end of his bachelorhood and the support of his closest friends as he begins this new chapter.
LA VÍBORA DE LA MAR

A traditional group dance where everyone forms a long line and passes under arches while the music plays. It symbolizes unity, community and the joining of two families as one.

LA LIGA

The groom removes the bride’s garter and either keeps it or tosses it to the single men. It’s a playful tradition that represents good luck, happiness and the passing of blessings to future marriages.

EL RAMO

The bride tosses her bouquet to the single women. It symbolizes love, happiness and new beginnings, with the hope that the next person to catch it will soon find love and marriage.

Explore Long Beach

Coming early or staying a little longer? The Marriott sits right on the downtown waterfront — most of Long Beach's best-known sights are a short walk or ride away, and Los Angeles makes an easy day trip. Distances are from the hotel; times vary with traffic, so check your route on the day.

Aquarium of the Pacific

About 0.7 miles · 15-minute walk

One of California's largest aquariums, on Rainbow Harbor — sharks, sea otters, penguins and touch pools across galleries for the whole Pacific Ocean. A perfect morning with kids before the festivities.

Directions

Shoreline Village & Rainbow Harbor

About 0.7 miles · waterfront walk

Colorful boardwalk shops, ice cream and waterfront dining around the marina, with harbor cruises and whale-watching boats leaving from the docks nearby — lovely at sunset.

Directions

The Queen Mary

About 1.5 miles · short drive or bus ride

The famous 1936 Cunard ocean liner, moored in Long Beach since 1967 and now a hotel and attraction with tours, exhibits and restaurants aboard. The free downtown Passport bus crosses the bay toward her.

Directions

Naples Canals & Belmont Shore

About 4 miles east · 12-minute drive

Stroll or glide by gondola through the Naples canals, then wander Second Street in Belmont Shore for cafés, boutiques and ice cream — Long Beach's beach-town side.

Directions

Catalina Island Day Trip

Ferry terminal about 1 mile from the hotel

Catalina Express ferries sail from downtown Long Beach to Avalon in about an hour — a full day of harbor views, shops and island scenery. Book ahead; ferries fill up on weekends.

Directions

Los Angeles Day Trips

Downtown LA about 25 miles north

The Metro A Line runs directly from Downtown Long Beach station to downtown Los Angeles — no driving or parking. By car, Disneyland in Anaheim is also about 25 miles away, roughly a 40-minute drive off-peak.

Downtown Long Beach Station

From Long Beach Airport (LGB)

4100 Donald Douglas Dr · about 6 miles · roughly 15–25 minutes by road

  • Easiest: taxi or rideshare (Uber/Lyft) from the curb outside the terminal — a short ride straight to the Marriott's Ocean Boulevard entrance.
  • Rental car: rental counters are at the airport; drive south toward downtown and the waterfront. Hotel self-parking is $25 per car.
  • Bus: Long Beach Transit buses serve the airport and run into downtown — the budget option if you're traveling light.

From Los Angeles Intl. (LAX)

About 22 miles · typically 35–75 minutes by road, longer at rush hour

  • Easiest: rideshare or taxi via the free LAX-it shuttle from your terminal, then I-405 South to I-710 South into downtown Long Beach.
  • Rental car: the same freeway route — I-405 S to I-710 S — allow extra time on weekday mornings and late afternoons.
  • Metro rail: from the LAX/Metro Transit Center, ride the C Line, transfer to the A Line, and ride to Downtown Long Beach station, a short walk from the hotel — about 1½ hours with transfers.

Flying in for the weekend? LGB is the close, easy airport — if your route offers it, it lands you about 6 miles from the hotel instead of about 22.
